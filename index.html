Typhoon Leon

Introduction 📔

Super Typhoon Leon, also known as Typhoon Kong-rey, made landfall in the Philippines in late October 2024 as the 21st named storm and third super typhoon of the 2024 Pacific typhoon season. It peaked on October 30, impacting Batanes and Cagayan Valley before spreading to Taiwan, China, South Korea, and Japan.

Causes and Formation 🌀

Typhoon Leon, originating in Guam, formed under ideal conditions such as moderate sea surface temperatures and little vertical wind shear. It was promoted to Kong-rey on October 25 and joined the Philippine Area of Responsibility on October 27 as Leon. It quickly developed, eventually becoming a super typhoon on October 30.

Impacts 📣

Leon killed three people and injured 690 in the Philippines, causing widespread damage to homes, electrical lines, and communication networks. Typhoons Kristine and Leon displaced over 10 million people in 19 locations. They also impacted the agricultural season, resulting in food shortages.

Mitigation Strategies 💭

  • Early Warning Systems: Improve forecasts and provide timely warnings to the public.
  • Mandatory Evacuations: Implemented in high-risk locations to ensure safety.
  • Infrastructure Resilience: Constructing and modifying buildings and infrastructure to resist typhoon-force winds and flooding.
  • Community Preparedness: Regular disaster drills and education efforts raise public awareness and preparation.
  • Environmental Management: Involves preserving and repairing natural barriers like mangroves and woodlands to mitigate storm surge and flooding concerns.
  • Climate Change Mitigation: Addressing underlying causes of increased typhoon intensity, such as global warming, via sustainable behaviors and legislation.

Conclusion 📚

Understanding typhoons like Leon is critical for designing mitigation techniques, investing in robust infrastructure, improving early warning systems, and encouraging community readiness to prevent negative consequences.
